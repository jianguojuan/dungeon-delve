Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 20, 2026

The short version

  • Your progress is kept in a file on your device. We have no servers, and we never receive it.
  • There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking. We don't sell or share anything about you.
  • The only thing the game sends over the internet is a report when it crashes or hits an error, to Unity, the maker of the game's engine, so we can fix it. It carries no name, email or account.
  • Apple and Google handle downloads and payments under their own privacy policies. We never see your card.
  • If you email us, we use what you send only to answer you.

1. Who we are

Dungeon Delve (“the game”) is made by one person, Zyrus Ian Crisostomo, in Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada, who is responsible for this policy and for any personal information it describes. In this policy “we” and “us” mean Zyrus Ian Crisostomo. You can reach us at zyrus@zyrusworks.com.

2. What the game keeps on your device

The game saves one file in its own private storage on your device. It holds:

WhatWhy
Your progress: your hero's level, gear, gems, crates, pets, eggs, skills, tomes, research, stages and keysSo you can carry on where you left off
The name you type and the picture you pick for your profileTo show them on your profile. Nobody else sees them
A record of what you have done in the game (for example enemies defeated and crates opened) and the day you startedTo show your record on your profile
The time the game was last open, and which daily rewards you have claimedTo pay you for time away, to give new keys each day, and for the shop's daily gift and Monthly Card
Your settings, such as vibration on or offSo the game remembers them

This file never leaves your device through the game. Deleting the game deletes it. Your device's own backup (iCloud on iPhone, Google's backup on Android) may include it, under your agreement with Apple or Google, and it may come back if you restore that backup.

3. What the game sends over the internet

Only reports of problems. The game is made with Unity, and while it runs, Unity's Diagnostics sends Unity a report when the game crashes, stops responding or hits an error, with some measures of how well it runs. A report holds:

  • what went wrong: the error, where in the game's code it happened, and what the game was doing just before;
  • about your device: its make and model, its operating system and version, and how much memory it has;
  • a random number the game makes when it is installed (the installation ID), so that several reports from one device can be counted as one. It is not your name, email or account, and it does not follow you to other apps.

We use the reports only to find and fix problems in the game. Unity handles them for us and on our behalf (for privacy law, Unity is our processor and we are the controller), under Unity's privacy policy, and keeps them for 90 days. They are sent encrypted. Nothing about your progress, your profile or anything you type is in them. The game has no servers of its own to talk to. Apple and Google may also collect crash reports from your device under their own policies, if you have allowed it in your device's settings; section 4 says what we see of those.

4. Apple and Google

You get the game, its updates and anything you buy in it through Apple's App Store or Google Play. They collect information about that under their own privacy policies (Apple, Google).

  • Purchases. Apple or Google take the payment. The store tells the game whether a purchase went through and what was bought, so that the game can give you your gems. We never receive your name, address or card details.
  • Statistics. Apple and Google show developers statistics such as the number of downloads, purchases and crashes by country and type of device. These don't tell us who you are. Apple shares crash and usage data with developers only if you have agreed to it in your device's settings.
  • Testers. If you test a pre-release version through TestFlight or Google Play testing, Apple or Google may show us the email address you were invited with, and any feedback, screenshots or crash reports you choose to send. We use these only to run the test and fix the game, and delete them when you ask.

5. When you contact us

If you email us or message the game's social media accounts, we receive your address or account name and what you write. We use it only to answer you and to fix the game, keep it only as long as that takes, and delete it when you ask.

6. What we don't do

  • We don't have accounts or sign-in, and we don't run servers.
  • The game has no ads, and doesn't track you across other apps or websites. It doesn't use your device's advertising ID, and the reports in section 3 are never used for advertising.
  • The game doesn't ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • We don't sell or share personal information, and we don't use it to profile you.

On Android the game asks for two permissions: to vibrate your phone on a good find, which you can switch off in the game's Settings, and to use the internet, for the reports in section 3.

7. Children

The game is not made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, for example in an email, contact us and we will delete it.

8. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, by writing to zyrus@zyrusworks.com. We answer within 30 days. Your game data is only on your device: to delete it, delete the game. The reports in section 3 are tied to a random installation ID and not to you, so we cannot pick out yours from your name or email; Unity deletes every report after 90 days.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom: we handle the reports in section 3, and the emails and tester details described above, because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the game working, answering you and running tests of the game.

If you are not happy with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or, in the European Union or the United Kingdom, to your country's data protection authority.

9. Where information is kept, and for how long

Your game data stays on your device until you delete the game. The reports in section 3 are kept by Unity for 90 days. Emails and tester details are kept in our email and the stores' developer tools for only as long as section 4 or 5 says. Unity, Apple and Google may store what they keep outside your country, for example in the United States.

10. Changes to this policy

If the game ever adds accounts, cloud saves, ads, analytics or anything else that changes what is collected, we will update this policy before that version comes out, and change the date at the top. For a big change we will also tell you in the game.
