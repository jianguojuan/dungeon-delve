Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 1, 2026

The short version

  • Your progress is saved on your device, and a copy is kept in the cloud with Unity Gaming Services so it can come back on a new phone.
  • Each player gets a random player ID. You can make an account with your email and a password, on Android sign in with Google Play Games and on iPhone with Game Center. We never see your password.
  • While the game is being tested, everyone plays as a guest: email accounts, Google Play Games and Game Center are switched off, and the shop is a test shop that charges nothing.
  • The game needs the internet to play. It asks Unity's servers for the time and its settings, so that it can't be fooled by a phone's clock.
  • The game has a World chat. What you write there is seen by other players with your game name and picture, and is kept by Unity's Vivox service for 7 days. If someone reports you, we see what was said, so we can keep the chat safe.
  • The game has a leaderboard: other players see your profile name and picture, your hero's power, level, highest stage, the gear it wears and the pets and skills you use on it, and can report a name.
  • The game has an Arena: other players fight a copy of your hero (the gear it wears and the pets and skills you use), played by our script on Unity's servers, whether you are online or not, and see your profile name, picture, power, level and highest stage, and how fights against you went.
  • The game counts how it is played (levels, stages, dungeon fights, how long you play) with Unity Analytics, to make the game better. In the EU, the UK and Switzerland it asks you first, and anyone can turn it off in Settings, under Privacy Choices.
  • The game has ads you choose to watch, for a reward, from Google's AdMob. Google uses your device's advertising ID for them. In Europe, the UK and Switzerland the game asks you first, and anyone can change their answer in Settings, under Privacy Choices. We don't sell anything about you.
  • When the game crashes or hits an error, it sends a report to Unity, the maker of the game's engine, so we can fix it.
  • You can delete your account and its cloud copy at any time, in the game's Settings or by asking us.
  • Apple and Google handle downloads and payments under their own privacy policies. We never see your card. When you buy something, our server checks the purchase with the store and keeps a record of it with your player ID.

1. Who we are

Dungeon Delve (“the game”) is made by one person, Zyrus Ian Crisostomo, in Canada, who is responsible for this policy and for any personal information it describes. In this policy “we” and “us” mean Zyrus Ian Crisostomo. You can reach us at zyrus@zyrusworks.com.

2. What the game keeps on your device

The game saves one file in its own private storage on your device. It holds:

WhatWhy
Your progress: your hero's level, gear, gems, crates, pets, eggs, skills, tomes, research, stages and keysSo you can carry on where you left off
The name you type and the picture you pick for your profileTo show them on your profile, and to other players on the leaderboard (section 3)
A record of what you have done in the game (for example enemies defeated and crates opened) and the day you startedTo show your record on your profile
The time the game was last open, and which daily rewards you have claimedTo pay you for time away, to give new keys each day, and for the shop's daily gift and the Delver Club
Your settings, such as vibration on or offSo the game remembers them
Whether you bought No Ads, the keys ads gave you today, and the minutes of XP boost you holdTo give each ad's reward, and only as often as the game allows
Your answer to the consent message (section 4), kept by Google's software in the game's own storageSo that you are not asked again, and the ads follow your answer

When the game replaces this file with the copy in the cloud (section 3), or starts a new game after a reset, it keeps the earlier file beside it on your device, so that it can be brought back if you ask us. The game also keeps your player ID and sign-in on your device, so that you are the same player the next time you play: if you signed in with an email, that email and a token that keeps you signed in, never your password. Deleting the game deletes all of this from your device. Your device's own backup (iCloud on iPhone, Google's backup on Android) may include it, under your agreement with Apple or Google.

3. Your player ID, signing in, the cloud copy and the chat

The game uses Unity Gaming Services, from Unity, the maker of the game's engine, for the player ID, the cloud copy and the chat.

  • Your player ID. The first time the game is online, Unity's Authentication service gives you a player ID: a random string that is not your name, email or phone number. You play as a guest under it. The game shows it in the profile's Settings, so that you can give it to us if you need help.
  • While the game is being tested. Everyone plays as a guest: the three ways of signing in below (Google Play Games, Game Center and an email account) are switched off, and the shop is a test shop that charges nothing, whose purchases our server records as test purchases (the Purchases item below). This policy describes them for when they are switched on.
  • Google Play Games (Android). If you are signed in to Google Play Games on your phone, the game signs you in with it, and a guest can sign in from Settings. Google gives the game a one-time code, which Unity checks with Google to link your Google Play Games player ID to your player ID, so that your progress follows your Google account to a new phone. The game asks Google for Play Games' basic sign-in only. We don't receive your email address, password, contacts or anything else from your Google account, and the game doesn't read or write your Google Drive. Your use of Google Play Games is under Google's privacy policy, and Google may show that you play Dungeon Delve in the Google Play Games app and in your Google activity.
  • Game Center (iPhone). If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, the game signs you in with it. Apple gives the game a player ID for our games (not your Apple ID or email) and a signed proof of it, which Unity checks with Apple to link that Game Center player ID to your player ID, so that your progress follows your Game Center account to a new iPhone. We don't receive your Apple ID, email address, friends or anything else from Apple. Your use of Game Center is under Apple's privacy policy.
  • An email account. You can make an account with your email address and a password, in the profile's Settings, on any phone. Google's Firebase Authentication keeps your email address and a protected (hashed) form of your password for us, checks them when you sign in, and sends the email that resets a forgotten password. Your player ID is then linked to that account, so that your progress comes back on any phone you sign in on. We use your email address only to sign you in, to reset your password and to answer you when you write to us. We never send you marketing, and we never see your password. Firebase handles it under Google's privacy policy and the Firebase data processing terms.
  • The leaderboard. While you are online, the game sends your hero's power to Unity's Leaderboards under your player ID, with your profile name and picture, your hero's level, the furthest stage you have cleared, the gear it wears (each piece and its level) and the pets and skills you have in use (each and its level): again whenever one of them changes, at most once a minute. Everyone who plays the game can see these on the leaderboard, and tap your name to see them on a card; they don't see your player ID, email or anything else. A player can block you, which hides you on their own device only, or report your name or report you for cheating: Unity's Moderation service then receives your player ID, theirs and the reason. We read reports to keep the leaderboard fair, and may take a player off it or remove a name that breaks our Terms.
  • The Arena. When you open the Arena (from the 3-10 boss on), Unity's servers keep, under your player ID: your league, the fights you used that day, your last 20 fights and the week's results; and in the week's group of up to 50 players, your profile name and picture, your hero's power, level and highest stage, your points and how many fights you won and lost. Every fight is played on Unity's servers by a script of ours, from the heroes as the server holds them, and kept with both heroes as they fought (the gear each wore and the pets and skills each used, with their levels) so that it can be played again. The players in your group see your name, picture, power, level and highest stage when you are offered to them, and fight a copy of your hero without you; a fight against you is kept in their record of fights too, with your name, picture and hero as they were, and in yours with theirs. Nobody sees your player ID, email or anything else.
  • The game's settings and the time. The game needs the internet to play. As it starts, each time you come back to it, and about once a minute while it runs, it asks Unity's Remote Config for the time and for the game's settings: the oldest version that may still be played, and whether the game is closed for maintenance. Each ask carries your player ID and the installation ID in section 5. Unity's servers also see your device's internet (IP) address, from which Remote Config may work out your rough region for that one answer. Remote Config keeps none of this after it answers.
  • Purchases. When you buy a gem pack, No Ads or the Delver Club, Google Play or the App Store tells the game that the purchase went through and gives it a purchase token (on iPhone, a transaction number). The game sends it to our server, a script on Unity's Cloud Code, which asks Google or Apple whether it was paid and keeps a record of it with your player ID in Unity's Cloud Save: the order number, what was bought, when, until when the Delver Club is paid for, and whether it was a test purchase. The game attaches a scrambled form of your player ID (a one-way hash, which can't be turned back into the ID) to each purchase in Google Play or the App Store, so that a purchase stays with the account that made it. We use the record to give you what you bought, to stop fake purchases and to answer you if you write to us about a purchase. Unity's purchasing software in the game also reports each purchase to Unity, with your player ID, a random installation ID, your device's model and system, and your country (worked out by Unity, not your precise location), for Unity to run the purchase service and to show us purchase statistics. With your consent, the game also counts what was bought and its price in its analytics (section 5). If Google or Apple refunds a purchase or the payment is reversed, our script learns of it from the store and marks it in your purchase record, and the game takes back what that purchase gave: its gems (your gems can go below zero if you had spent them), a month of the Delver Club's gems, or No Ads.
  • The cloud copy. While you play, a copy of the file in section 2 is sent to Unity's Cloud Save and kept under your player ID: at most every few minutes, and when you leave the game. It holds the same things as section 2, with a count of how many times the game has saved. When the game finds two copies of your progress that disagree (for example from two phones), it keeps the one further on and keeps the other aside, so that we can bring it back if you ask; a copy kept aside on your device is sent to the cloud as well.
  • World chat. When the game is online it joins the World chat, which Unity's Vivox service runs. Vivox gets your player ID, your device's internet (IP) address, a rough idea of where you are worked out from it (used only to connect you to a nearby server, and not kept), and some measures of how well the connection works. When you send a message, the other players in the chat see it with the game name and the picture you picked, and Vivox keeps the message, your player ID, the name and the picture for 7 days, so that players who come in later see the latest messages. Vivox's Safe Text filter checks what is written for bad words before others see it. Nobody sees your name until you send a message.
  • Reports, blocks and mutes. If you report a message, Unity's Moderation service gets your player ID, the player ID of who you reported, and what was said in your chat room in the 15 minutes before. We read reports in Unity's dashboard to decide whether to remove messages, or to mute or ban a player for a while or for good. A ban turns the player ID off: it can't sign in, use the chat or keep its cloud copy until the ban ends. Who you block and mute is kept on your device, which hides those players' messages from you.
  • Help and fair play. When you write to us, we look up your cloud copy by your player ID, and can bring back a copy kept aside or give back what you lost: the game takes it the next time it starts. We keep short notes about what we did for an account, and about breaking the fair play rules of our terms, on our own computer. If we ban an account for cheating, its cloud copy is deleted and a ban mark is left in its place: the next time the game is opened, it deletes the progress on that device and the account itself, and says so. A game changed to get round this has its player ID turned off. We keep a copy of a banned account's cloud copy on our own computer for 30 days, in case the ban was a mistake.
  • Checks against cheating. Each time the game sends its cloud copy, a script on Unity's servers compares it with the last one it saw of the same game, with how much time has passed and with your purchase record, and notes anything playing and buying cannot explain (gems or crates that grew too fast, an item your hero's level can't have yet). The game also checks itself while it runs: if a number such as your gems is changed in its memory by another app, or the game's time runs faster than the server's (a "speed hack"), it notes this in its save, which goes up with the cloud copy. On an Android device, the game also asks Google Play to vouch for it when you sign in and every hour (Google's Play Integrity): Google Play looks at the game's own details (its name, version and signing certificate), whether it was installed from Google Play for your Google account, and a proof from your device that it is genuine and unmodified, and gives the game a sealed answer, tied to your player ID and the hour by a scrambled code. A script on Unity's servers has Google open it, and notes whether the game is unmodified, installed from Google Play and on a genuine device; a game that isn't is noted as above. Google says it uses this data only to check the game and the device, encrypts it, shares it with no one else and deletes it after a set time (its Play Integrity terms). These notes are kept with your player ID on Unity's servers, where only we can read them. A noted account's power is kept off the leaderboard until we have looked; we look at the notes and the cloud copy ourselves before we act, and nothing is taken from you by the check alone. The leaderboard's score is written by a script on Unity's servers too, which refuses a power no hero of your level could have.

Unity handles your player ID, the cloud copy and the purchase record for us and on our behalf, and Google's Firebase your email account (for privacy law, each is our processor and we are the controller), under Unity's privacy policy and its data processing terms. They are sent encrypted. Unity's servers also receive your device's internet (IP) address when the game connects, as any online service does. We use your player ID, your email account and the cloud copy only to keep your progress and bring it back, and to help you when you write to us; the purchase record only as the Purchases item above says; and your player ID and chat messages also to run the chat and keep it safe. They are kept until you delete your account (section 10), chat messages for 7 days, and reports and bans as long as section 11 says.

4. Ads

Some rewards in the game can be doubled, or a dungeon key earned, by watching an ad. Ads only play when you tap a button that says so; none play by themselves. If you buy No Ads, those buttons give their reward straight away and no ad plays.

The ads come from Google's AdMob. When an ad is asked for or plays, Google's Mobile Ads software in the game sends Google:

  • your device's advertising ID (on Android, Google's advertising ID and the app set ID; on iPhone, Apple's only if you allow tracking), which you can reset or delete in your device's settings;
  • your device's internet (IP) address, from which Google works out roughly where you are (your country or city, not your exact place);
  • about your device and the game: its make, model and operating system, its language, and the game's name and version;
  • how the ads are used: which ads were shown, tapped and watched to their reward, and how the ads software is working.

Google uses this to choose and show ads, to measure them, to stop fraud and to pay us. If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the game first shows Google's consent message, which lists what your data would be used for and Google's ad partners. If you don't consent, Google still shows ads, but not ones chosen for you (it still uses some data to show an ad, count it and stop fraud, as the law there allows). You can change your answer at any time in the game's Settings, under Privacy Choices. Elsewhere, you can turn off personalised ads in your device's settings.

Google handles this as its own controller, under Google's privacy policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services. You can turn off personalised ads for your whole device in its settings (Android: Settings, Google, Ads; iPhone: Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking), and see or change how Google chooses ads at My Ad Center.

We don't receive your advertising ID from the ads: AdMob shows us only totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned, by country. One thing goes the other way, so that a reward is only given for an ad really watched: before an ad plays, the game tags it with your player ID (section 3) and a one-time code, and once you have watched it, Google sends those two back to our server with a signature, through a small service of ours that Cloudflare runs for us, which notes the code with your player ID on Unity's servers and keeps nothing itself. The note is kept for up to 7 days, and the codes already paid as part of your purchase and game records until your account is deleted. Nothing else of sections 3 and 5 (the cloud copy, the chat, the leaderboard, the reports) is given to the ads.

5. Reports of problems, and how the game is played

Reports of problems

The game is made with Unity, and while it runs, Unity's Diagnostics sends Unity a report when the game crashes, stops responding or hits an error, with some measures of how well it runs. A report holds:

  • what went wrong: the error, where in the game's code it happened, and what the game was doing just before;
  • about your device: its make and model, its operating system and version, and how much memory it has;
  • a random number the game makes when it is installed (the installation ID), so that several reports from one device can be counted as one. It is not your name, email, account or player ID, and it does not follow you to other apps.

We use the reports only to find and fix problems in the game. Unity handles them for us and on our behalf, as our processor, under Unity's privacy policy, and keeps them for 90 days. They are sent encrypted. Nothing about your progress, your profile or anything you type is in them. Apple and Google may also collect crash reports from your device under their own policies, if you have allowed it in your device's settings; section 6 says what we see of those.

How the game is played (analytics)

To see how many people play, how many come back, and where the game is too hard or too slow, the game tells Unity Analytics, Unity's analytics service, about your play:

  • what happens in the game: each level you take; the first time you clear each stage, and how many tries a boss took; how each dungeon fight ended (the dungeon, its difficulty, won, lost or left, how long it took and how much health you had left); the first time you find each part of the game, such as a dungeon, pets, skills, research, the shop or a new rarity; which page was open when you left the game; and once a day, what you did the day before in numbers (minutes played, levels, stages, crates opened, flying chests, dungeon wins, eggs, tomes, research, gems earned and spent, quests, shop visits);
  • once the game sells things for real money: what was bought and its price, never your card;
  • when and how long you play, the game's version, your device's make, model, operating system and language, and your country, which Unity works out from your internet (IP) address;
  • a random installation ID made by Unity, the identifier your device gives the game (not its advertising ID), and your player ID from section 3.

If you are in the European Union, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, nothing is sent until you say yes when the game asks. Anywhere else it is on from the start, and you can turn it off in Settings, under Privacy Choices, which stops it at once. We use it only to understand how the game is played and to make it better, never for advertising. Unity handles it for us and on our behalf, as our processor, under Unity's privacy policy, and keeps the events for 13 months. It is sent encrypted.

6. Apple and Google

You get the game, its updates and anything you buy in it through Apple's App Store or Google Play. They collect information about that under their own privacy policies (Apple, Google).

  • Purchases. Apple or Google take the payment. The store tells the game whether a purchase went through and what was bought, and our server checks it with the store (section 3), so that the game can give you your gems. We never receive your name, address or card details.
  • Statistics. Apple and Google show developers statistics such as the number of downloads, purchases and crashes by country and type of device. These don't tell us who you are. Apple shares crash and usage data with developers only if you have agreed to it in your device's settings.
  • Testers. If you test a pre-release version through TestFlight or Google Play testing, Apple or Google may show us the email address you were invited with, and any feedback, screenshots or crash reports you choose to send. We use these only to run the test and fix the game, and delete them when you ask.

7. When you contact us

If you email us or message the game's social media accounts, we receive your address or account name and what you write. We use it only to answer you and to fix the game, keep it only as long as that takes, and delete it when you ask.

8. What we don't do

  • We don't run servers of our own: your player ID, cloud copy, purchase record, leaderboard entry, chat messages, reports and analytics are kept by Unity for us, and an email account by Google's Firebase, as section 3 says. The script that checks purchases runs on Unity's servers. Our notes about accounts, and the copy of a banned account's progress, are on our own computer, which only we use.
  • We don't track you across other apps or websites ourselves. The ads in section 4 are Google's, and nothing in sections 3 and 5 is ever used for them.
  • The game doesn't ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • We don't sell or share personal information, and we don't use it to profile you.

On Android the game asks for these permissions: to vibrate your phone on a good find, which you can switch off in the game's Settings; to use the internet, which the game needs to play: for sign-in, the game's settings and the time, the cloud copy, checking purchases, the chat, the ads, and the reports and analytics in section 5; to see whether your phone is connected (to a network or Wi-Fi), so the chat can reconnect; to read the advertising ID, for the ads in section 4; and Google Play's billing, to buy in the game. The chat is text only: the game doesn't ask for your microphone.

9. Children

The game is not made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Its World chat lets players talk with people they don't know, which is one more reason it is for 13 and over. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

10. Your rights, and deleting your account

Wherever you live, you can ask us what personal information we hold about you, ask for a copy of it, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, by writing to zyrus@zyrusworks.com. We answer within 30 days. Tell us your player ID from the game's Settings, so that we can find your cloud copy.

To delete your account yourself, open your profile in the game (tap your name at the top left), go to Settings and tap Delete Account. That takes your name, picture and power off the leaderboard, takes you off the Arena (your name and picture off its groups, so that no one is offered you, and your fights off your own record), deletes your purchase record, your cloud copy and anything kept aside with it, then your player ID and the email account you signed in with, then your progress on that device, and the game starts again as a new player. It also asks Unity to delete the analytics about you, which Unity does within 30 days, and turns analytics off on that device until you say yes again. It can't be undone. If you can't open the game, our deletion page says how to ask us instead. Reports of problems (section 5) are tied to an installation ID and not to your player ID, so we can't pick out yours; Unity deletes every report after 90 days. Chat messages you sent stay in the chat until Vivox deletes them, 7 days after each was sent. A fight you had against another player stays in their record of fights, with your name, picture and hero as they were, until it is one of their 20 oldest fights; and a small record of your Arena league and the fights you used that day, without your name, picture or fights, stays so that leaving can't be used to fight again. Reports you made or that were made about you, any ban, and the checks against cheating, are kept after you delete your account, as long as section 11 says, so that a banned player can't simply start again. So is a record that pairs a scrambled form of each purchase's Google token or App Store transaction with your player ID, so that a purchase can't be paid out twice. Deleting your account doesn't refund a purchase or cancel the Delver Club, and Apple and Google keep their own records of what you bought. Unity keeps its own reports of purchases for its purchase service as its privacy policy says; when you ask us to delete your data, we ask Unity to delete them too.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom: we keep your player ID and cloud copy, and run the chat, to provide the game you asked to play; we count how the game is played (section 5) only with your consent, which you can take back at any time in Settings, under Privacy Choices; we handle chat reports and bans because we have a legitimate interest in keeping players safe; and we handle the reports in section 5, emails and tester details because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the game working, answering you and running tests of the game. Google uses your data for ads chosen for you only with your consent, which its consent message asks for; for ads that are not chosen for you, and to count them and stop fraud, Google relies on its own legitimate interest.

If you are not happy with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or, in the European Union or the United Kingdom, to your country's data protection authority.

11. Where information is kept, and for how long

The file in section 2 stays on your device until you delete the game. Your player ID, cloud copy, purchase record, leaderboard entry and Arena record are kept by Unity, and an email account by Google's Firebase, until you delete your account or ask us to. Chat messages are kept by Vivox for 7 days. The Arena's weekly groups and the records of fights are kept by Unity while the game runs, your name and picture taken off them when you delete your account; a fight against another player stays in their record until it is one of their 20 oldest. Reports from the chat and the leaderboard, and bans, are kept for up to 2 years after the report or the end of the ban, to keep the chat safe, then deleted; so are the checks against cheating, for up to 2 years after the last one, and the record that pairs each purchase with your player ID, for up to 2 years after the purchase. Our notes about an account are kept as long as the account is, and those about a ban for as long as the ban lasts; the copy of a banned account's progress for 30 days. The reports in section 5 are kept by Unity for 90 days, and the analytics in section 5 for 13 months. Emails and tester details are kept in our email and the stores' developer tools only as long as sections 6 and 7 say. Google keeps what the ads send it as its own policy says. Unity, Apple and Google may store what they keep outside your country, for example in the United States.

12. Changes to this policy

If the game ever adds anything else that changes what is collected, we will update this policy before that version comes out, and change the date at the top. For a big change we will also tell you in the game.
